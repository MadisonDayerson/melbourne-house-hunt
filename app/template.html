Found a place on realestate.com.au, Domain or an agent's site? Add it here so everyone can vote on it. The page finds the address on the map and works out its walkability, transport, noise, air and safety scores straight away.

Added by the group

Each listing gets seven scores out of 100. The overall score is an average you control under What matters to us: each score is multiplied by its setting (×0 to ×5) before averaging, so ×2 counts twice as much as ×1 and ×0 leaves a score out. Distances are measured from the listing's map pin. Walking distance is estimated as straight-line distance × 1.3, a typical detour factor for Melbourne's grid streets, at 80 m per minute.

Walkability

Based on Plan Melbourne's 20-minute neighbourhood: you should be able to meet most daily needs within an 800 m walk, 10 minutes there and 10 back. We check nine needs: supermarket, fresh food (greengrocer, butcher or bakery), pharmacy, GP, café or restaurant, park, gym or pool, library or community centre, and a train within 800 m or a tram within 600 m. The walkability score is half the share of needs met and half the listing's Walk Score®.

Public transport

Uses the walking-distance standards in Victoria's planning rules for new neighbourhoods (Clause 56.04-1): a bus stop within 400 m, a tram stop within 600 m and a train station within 800 m. Trains count most, then trams, then buses, because they run more often and carry more people. Blended 50/50 with the listing's Transit Score®. Bus frequency isn't captured, so check the PTV timetable for your actual routes.

Quiet

Australian noise maps aren't published per address, so distance to the main noise sources stands in for decibels. The World Health Organization's environmental noise guidelines (2018) name road traffic, rail and aircraft as the sources most linked to sleep disturbance and health effects. Points come off for:

Clean air

Traffic-related air pollution (fine particles, NO₂) is highest beside busy roads and falls off sharply over the first 150–300 m. Living within about 100 m of a major road is linked to asthma in children, worse lung function and heart disease. Victoria has no buffer rule for homes, so we use the 150 m guideline that California applies to schools. Points come off for a freeway within 150 m (40), 150–300 m (20) or 300–500 m (8), and a main road within 50 m (25) or 50–150 m (12). This score doesn't cover wood-heater smoke in winter or bushfire smoke, which affect the whole region. Outer suburbs in the hills and valleys (Yarra Ranges, parts of Casey and Cardinia) can trap wood smoke on still winter nights. Check EPA AirWatch for live readings from the nearest EPA monitoring station.

Safety

Recorded offences per 1,000 residents in the suburb, from the Crime Statistics Agency Victoria (year ending June 2026), divided by the suburb's 2021 Census population. Victoria overall is 86.8 per 1,000. The score is 75 at the state average, 100 at half of it and 25 at double it. Things to keep in mind:

  • Suburbs with big shopping centres, hospitals, nightlife or a police station record more offences (for example Melbourne CBD, Dandenong, Frankston, Ringwood, Preston with Northland, and Heidelberg with the Austin). Shoplifting and incidents at those sites inflate the rate without making the streets less safe for residents.
  • Population is from 2021, so fast-growing suburbs (Wyndham, Melton, Casey, Hume and Whittlesea growth areas) look worse than they are.
  • Suburbs with fewer than 1,000 residents, mostly industrial or rural, get no safety score: their rates swing too much to compare.
  • Burglary per 1,000 (shown in each listing's details) is the figure most relevant to a household.

Agency

How the agency managing the place treats renters, from two public sources:

  • Reviews on ProductReview.com.au. Most offices don't have their own page, so franchise offices (Ray White, Barry Plant, Harcourts, LJ Hooker…) get their network's rating and are labelled that way. Reviews come from renters, landlords, buyers and sellers. Review sites draw unhappy customers, so 2–3 stars is typical.
  • Consumer Affairs Victoria news releases and court outcomes since 2010 about enforcement against estate agents: bond and trust-account offences, rental-law breaches, bans and VCAT or court action. We search those stories for each agency's name, and each match was checked by hand. Records about an individual employee, where CAV says the agency wasn't aware, are shown but not counted.

Score: the star rating mapped from 1★ = 0 to 5★ = 100, pulled towards 2.5★ when there are few reviews, so one glowing review can't top the table. An agency fined or convicted by CAV loses 30 points; one facing proceedings that haven't been decided loses 15. Agencies with neither reviews nor CAV records get no score, and the overall score leaves this part out for them. Before signing, it's still worth looking up the specific office on Google and asking friends who've rented through it.

When it was built

Listings don't include a build year, so we read each listing's description. In order: a stated year ("built in 2016"), phrases like "brand new" or "only 4 years old", then the style described (Victorian, Edwardian, Californian bungalow, art deco, 1960s brick). If the description says nothing and the suburb is almost entirely post-2000 estates (Tarneit, Clyde North, Mickleham…), it's marked as a guess. Otherwise it's Unknown. Hover over the build year on a card to see the words it came from. Age isn't part of the overall score, but older homes are often draughty and harder to heat, so check heating and insulation at the inspection.

Price

100 at the hunt's minimum weekly rent, falling to 0 at its maximum. The rent range in the filters starts from the hunt's budget; you can narrow it for yourself without changing it for everyone.

Hunts, budgets and splitting the rent

Each hunt has its own people, budget, bedrooms, votes and notes, shared by everyone in it. Under Settings:

  • Who's paying. One row per person, or per couple sharing a room. Each row needs a bedroom, so the minimum bedrooms follows the number of rows unless you change it.
  • The split. Equally per person: a couple pays for two. Equally per bedroom: a couple shares one room's cost. Custom: any percentages, scaled to add up to 100%.
  • Salary (optional). Listings show that person's share as a percentage of their pay. 30% of pre-tax pay is the usual line for "rental stress" in Australia; on $100,000 a year that's about $580 a week. Take-home pay is estimated with 2026–27 income tax rates and the 2% Medicare levy, leaving out HELP repayments, offsets and salary sacrifice.

If your name in the hunt matches a row, listings show your share; otherwise they show the range per person.

Rooming houses and student accommodation

Some listings are a single room rather than a whole home. These are hidden unless you untick Hide rooming houses & student accommodation. A listing counts as one when its description says it's a rooming or boarding house, student accommodation (or is run by a student-housing operator such as UniLodge, Scape, Iglu, Urbanest or Yugo), or a room in a shared house ("room for rent", "private room", "furnished room", "co-living"). Hover over the red label on a card to see the words it came from. Listings that don't say so in their description can slip through.

Where the data comes from

  • Listings: rent.com.au public search results for 539 suburbs in Melbourne's 31 metropolitan councils, refreshed every morning: studios to 2 bedrooms up to $900 a week, 3 bedrooms up to $1,400 and 4+ bedrooms up to $1,900. Places listed only on realestate.com.au or Domain won't appear unless you add them.
  • Walk Score® and Transit Score®: supplied with each listing by rent.com.au.
  • Build age and features: the listing's own description on rent.com.au.
  • Agency reviews: ProductReview.com.au. Regulator records: Consumer Affairs Victoria news.
  • Shops, services, parks, stops, roads and rail: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Some places may be missing or out of date.
  • Crime: Crime Statistics Agency Victoria, recorded offences by suburb, year ending June 2026.
  • Regions: Inner city (Melbourne, Yarra, Port Phillip, Stonnington), North (Merri-bek, Darebin, Banyule, Moonee Valley, Hume, Whittlesea, Nillumbik), West (Maribyrnong, Hobsons Bay, Brimbank, Wyndham, Melton), East (Boroondara, Manningham, Whitehorse, Maroondah, Knox, Yarra Ranges), South-east (Monash, Glen Eira, Greater Dandenong, Casey, Cardinia), Bayside & peninsula (Bayside, Kingston, Frankston, Mornington Peninsula).
  • Population: ABS Census 2021.
  • Planning standards: Plan Melbourne 2017–2050 (Direction 5, 20-minute neighbourhoods); Victoria Planning Provisions Clause 56.04-1; WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines for the European Region (2018).
